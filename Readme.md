## Learning and Optimization for Process and Infrastructure Decisions

**How should technologies, processes, and infrastructure be designed and transitioned together under changing and uncertain conditions?**

I develop models and optimization methods to connect technology performance with process design and infrastructure planning. My work combines physics-informed learning, process simulation, and mathematical optimization to understand how engineering choices depend on one another across scales and time.

I am a postdoctoral researcher working with Professor Marianthi Ierapetritou at the University of Delaware. I received my Ph.D. in Chemical and Biomolecular Engineering from the University of Connecticut in 2025, advised by Professor George M. Bollas. I earned my M.S. and B.S. in Energy and Chemical Engineering from Incheon National University, where I worked with Professor Jiyong Kim.

## Research Vision

Emerging technologies must operate within larger systems shaped by resource availability, existing assets, and changing demand. A promising laboratory result may not lead to a viable commercial process, and an efficient process may not be the best choice for a regional supply network. Infrastructure investments can also constrain which technologies can be deployed later.

My long-term goal is to develop **adaptive decision systems** that connect learning with optimization across these scales. The research program I am developing centers on three connected questions:

- **What should we learn before scaling up?**  
  Use commercial design decisions to identify which uncertain scale-up relationships matter and prioritize measurements that help distinguish competing designs.

- **What process information do infrastructure decisions need?**  
  Preserve the process relationships needed for reliable system decisions, and translate system needs into quantitative targets for process improvement.

- **When should a transition plan change?**  
  Update planning models as new information arrives and identify when the expected benefits of revising a pathway justify the cost of changing course.

These questions form a feedback loop: experimental and operating information improves process models; process models inform infrastructure decisions; and system-level decisions guide further learning, process development, and plan revision.

Initial applications will focus on **sustainable fuels, circular resource systems, and low-carbon infrastructure**.

## Selected Research

### Learning Interpretable Models of Evolving Physical Systems

Tool degradation accumulates over time, but sensor measurements do not directly reveal the underlying wear state. During my Ph.D., I developed a physics-informed recursive symbolic regression framework that combines prior state estimates with physical knowledge in explicit mathematical expressions. The framework predicts tool wear and remaining useful life across different machining conditions.

This work provides a foundation for my broader interest in models that learn from evolving physical information.

### Connecting Process Development with Integrated Design and Sustainability Assessment

Laboratory performance alone does not establish whether an emerging process will be economically and environmentally viable at scale. In my postdoctoral research, I connect experimental data with process models, techno-economic analysis, and life cycle assessment.

For food-waste conversion to volatile fatty acids, I developed surrogate models for fermentation and recovery and embedded them in a joint optimization framework. Across the studied feedstock compositions, joint optimization reduced total annual cost by **22–45% relative to two-step optimization**, while maintaining or improving recovery yield.

In solvent-based nylon recycling, I investigate how solvent use, separation requirements, and energy demand affect scale-up feasibility.

### Designing Energy Supply Networks and Infrastructure Investment Pathways

Energy infrastructure decisions link technology selection, facility location, capacity, transportation, and investment timing. I developed a multi-period optimization model for national renewable energy infrastructure planning, showing how resource geography and transportation costs shape deployment patterns across energy carriers.

My current work extends this systems perspective to sustainable aviation fuel, connecting seasonal feedstock availability, existing facilities, storage, transportation, and airport demand to deployment decisions.
